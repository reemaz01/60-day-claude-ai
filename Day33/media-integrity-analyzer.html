Welcome

Train your eye to spot manipulation before it works on you.

This isn't a quiz. There's no failing. You'll look at real-feeling headlines and posts, form your own gut reaction, and then we'll pull back the curtain on exactly how they were built to influence you.

Every day, headlines and posts are engineered — sometimes subtly, sometimes not — to grab attention, trigger emotion, or push a click. Most of it works because we react instinctively, without pausing. Learning to notice how and why something pulls at you is the single most useful media literacy skill there is.

Concept · Headline Detective

Headlines are sales pitches, not summaries.

A headline's job is to get you to click — and the article's job is often just to justify that click after the fact. Writers reach for absolute words ("cures," "destroys," "secretly"), impossible timeframes ("overnight," "instantly"), and vague authority ("scientists say," "experts warn") because those phrases trigger curiosity and urgency, whether or not the underlying facts support them.

Why it matters: Most people only read the headline. If the headline overstates the story, a distorted idea spreads even among people who never open the article. Being able to instantly spot inflated language protects you from misinformation before it even starts.

In this challenge, you'll read a headline and its article. First, react honestly — would you actually click it? Then hunt through the article for the phrases that don't hold up.

Concept · Emotion Detector

If it made you feel something fast, that's the point.

Posts designed to spread aren't optimized for accuracy — they're optimized for a reaction. Fear, outrage, guilt, and urgency all get people to react before they think, which is exactly what drives shares, comments, and sales. This isn't accidental; specific word choices are selected because they reliably produce specific emotions in specific audiences.

Why it matters: Once you notice you're feeling something strong while reading, you can pause and ask, "who benefits from me feeling this way right now?" That one habit alone can defuse most manipulative content.
