# My Claude Usage Strategy
Student · Coding + Deep Research · Daily User


## Recommended Primary Model

Claude Sonnet 4.6  is your daily driver. It strikes the best balance between intelligence and speed, handling the vast majority of what a coding and research-focused student needs every day.



## Why This Model Fits Me

As a student who codes daily and needs deep research support, you need a model that can actually reason through problems — not just retrieve facts. Sonnet is smart enough to debug real logic errors, explain academic papers with nuance, help you understand new frameworks, and assist with project planning. It does all of this without the slowness of Opus or the shallowness of Haiku. For a daily user, it's the most sustainable choice.



## When to Use Haiku

Use Haiku when speed matters more than depth and the task is genuinely simple:

- Checking a syntax error quickly
- Looking up a simple definition or formula
- Asking a quick yes/no question while studying
- Generating boilerplate code you already understand
- Repetitive, low-stakes tasks you need done fast

Rule of thumb: If you could Google it in 10 seconds, Haiku is enough.



## When to Use Sonnet

Use Sonnet for the bulk of your daily work — roughly 80% of everything:

- Debugging logic errors and reviewing your code
- Summarising and analysing research papers
- Learning and understanding new programming concepts or frameworks
- Planning projects and assignments
- Exam preparation at a solid level of depth
- Writing explanations, notes, or study guides

Rule of thumb:If it requires reasoning, explanation, or synthesis, Sonnet is your default.



## When to Use Opus

Reserve Opus for your highest-stakes, most complex tasks:

- Designing complex algorithms or data structures
- Thesis or dissertation-level research and synthesis
- Working across large, multi-file codebases
- Problems that Sonnet genuinely struggled with
- Deep academic analysis where every nuance matters

Rule of thumb: Pull out Opus when Sonnet's answer feels incomplete or when the stakes are too high to settle for "good enough."


## Recommended Effort Level

### Low
Use this for flash lookups, yes/no checks, and syntax questions. Rarely needed — Haiku at Standard effort usually serves the same purpose better.

### Standard
Your everyday default. Use this for regular coding help, concept explanations, reading paper summaries, and most study tasks. It covers the majority of your daily interactions with Claude.

### High
Use this when you need thorough, careful answers — complex bug hunting, nuanced research synthesis, multi-step problem solving, and exam prep where you need detail, not just a surface answer. You should be using High effort fairly regularly given your research focus.

### Max
Save this for your most demanding work: dissertation chapters, architecture decisions for large projects, and bugs you genuinely cannot solve any other way. It is slower and should be used sparingly — not as a default.



## My Personalized Claude Workflow

| Task | Best Model | Best Effort | Reason |

| Fixing a syntax error | Haiku | Standard | Trivial task — speed wins |
| Debugging logic errors | Sonnet | High | Needs real reasoning, not pattern matching |
| Learning a new framework | Sonnet | Standard | Good explanations without overkill |
| Researching a paper or topic | Sonnet | High | Nuanced synthesis benefits from more depth |
| Building a full project | Opus | High | Multi-file context needs the strongest model |
| Dissertation or thesis prep | Opus | Max | Highest stakes — use the best available |
| Code concept explanation | Sonnet | Standard | Conversational depth without overkill |
| Exam practice questions | Sonnet | High | You want thorough, not just fast |



## Biggest Mistakes to Avoid

Using Haiku for complex research. It will oversimplify, miss important nuance, or confidently give you an incomplete answer. The time you save is not worth the quality you lose.

Using Opus for everything. You will burn through your usage limits on tasks that Sonnet handles just as well. Save Opus for when it is genuinely necessary.

Setting Max effort by default. It is slower and the gains are marginal on straightforward tasks. Use Standard most of the time and step up only when the problem demands it.

Copying code without understanding it. Claude is at its most valuable as a teacher. Always ask it to explain what the code does and why — not just to generate it. This is how you actually improve.

Writing vague prompts. "Fix my code" gives Claude almost nothing to work with. Always include the error message, what you expected to happen, what actually happened, and any relevant context. Better input means significantly better output.

## Final Recommendation

If you could use only one model and one effort level for most of your work, the answer is clear:

Claude Sonnet 4.6 at High effort.
For a student who codes daily and needs deep research support, this combination gives you genuine reasoning ability, reliable depth, and enough speed to stay in your flow. High effort ensures your answers are thorough rather than surface-level — which matters when you are learning, debugging, or synthesising research. It covers roughly 85% of your needs at a level of quality that is hard to improve on. Step up to Opus with Max effort only when the task is truly demanding and the stakes are at their highest.
